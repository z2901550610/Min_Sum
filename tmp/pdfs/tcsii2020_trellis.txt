Optimized Trellis-Based Min-Max Decoder
for NB-LDPC Codes
Jing Tian , Suwen Song, Jun Lin, Senior Member, IEEE, and Zhongfeng Wang , Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—Non-binary low-density parity-check (NB-LDPC)
codes outperform their binary counterparts in many cases.
However, an NB-LDPC decoder usually requires excessive hard-
ware resources and memory consumption. The trellis-based
min-max decoding algorithm (TMMA), a well-known algorithm
proposed in recent years, achieves good tradeoff between decod-
ing performance and hardware complexity. Note that the check
node processing unit (CNU) occupies the most hardware con-
sumption. Based on the TMMA, many simpliﬁcations for the
CNU have been developed with slight performance loss. The
current TMMA with L truncations (L-TMMA) is promising for
higher hardware efﬁciency than others. In this brief, based on
the L-TMMA, we propose a new CNU design by incorporat-
ing algorithmic transformation and architectural optimization to
further reduce the hardware complexity and thereby the criti-
cal path without any performance degradation. Synthesis results
show that the proposed design achieves the lowest hardware con-
sumption and the highest clock frequency with a small latency
compared to the state-of-the-arts. Speciﬁcally, it saves more than
1/3 hardware resources compared with its original one.
Index Terms—Error correction codes, non-binary LDPC codes,
iterative decoding, min-max decoding algorithm, low-complexity
decoding, VLSI.
I. I NTRODUCTION
N
ON-BINARY low-density parity-check (NB-LDPC)
codes, ﬁrstly proposed by Davey and Mackay in 1998 [1],
have attracted much attention due to their lower error ﬂoor,
better error-correcting performance, and greater ability of burst
error correction than their binary counterparts, especially for
short and moderate code lengths. Nevertheless, the excessive
computations in the decoding form the bottleneck all the time
for their practical applications. The optimal decoding is per-
formed by the q-ary sum-product algorithm (QSPA) [1] but
it is unpractical because of the huge computational complex-
ity and computation instability. In order to deal with these
problems, many sub-optimal decoding algorithms like the
extended min-sum algorithm (EMSA) [2] and min-max algo-
rithm (MMA) [3], are proposed. However, their computational

Recently, the trellis-based decoding algorithms for NB-
LDPC codes have been widely used since they can greatly
improve the degree of parallelism with good decoding
performance and low computational complexity by shift-
ing operations of the check node processing (CNP) from
normal domain to delta domain. At the beginning, the simpli-
ﬁed min-sum algorithm (SMSA) [4] and trellis-based EMSA
(TEMSA) [5] were proposed. For the SMSA [4], Wang et al.
presented an approximation for the CNP and conducted the
CNP by parallelizing d
c separate conﬁgurations for a check
node after computing the 1-order conﬁguration (just one devi-
ation). The TEMSA adds an extra column for the CNP to help
compute in parallel. For the two algorithms, a trade-off can
be made between the error performance and computational
complexity by choosing a suitable number of deviations.
The trellis-based MMA (TMMA) [6] is generalized from
the TEMSA by replacing the addition operation with the
comparison operation and reducing the deviations from three
to two. Then, many simpliﬁed versions of the TMMA are
developed [7]–[12]. Lacruz et al. [7] proposed the one
minimum only TMMA (OMO-TMMA), in which the two-
minimum ﬁnder is substituted by a one-minimum ﬁnder and
an estimator for the second minimum. They compressed the
messages exchanged from the check node to the variable node
in [8], from q×d
c×w bits to 2×(q− 1)×(w+⌈log dc⌉)+dc×p
bits. In [9], they further reduced the exchanged messages
to 2 × (q − 1) ×⌈ log d
c⌉+ (q + 1) × w + (dc + 2) × p
bits. In [10], they greatly compressed these messages to
L × (w + p + 2 ×⌈ log dc⌉) + (q − 1) × w + dc × p bits, where
L ≪ q. This new algorithm is simply called L-TMMA. When
L is chosen appropriately, a good decoding performance can
be achieved. Thi and Lee [11] processed the check node in
a different way – by adding two extra columns for the CNP
and replacing the two-minimum ﬁnder with the one-minimum
ﬁnder, which could slightly improve the decoding performance
but still needs the same number of exchanged messages as
that of [8]. Inspired by the relaxed MMA (RMMA) [13], they
developed the basic-set TMMA (BS-TMMA) and reduced the
exchanged messages to p×(w+p+⌈log d
c⌉)+(q−1)×w+dc×p
bits while the algorithm suffers an observable performance
degradation.
Considering the decoding performance and hardware efﬁ-
ciency, in this brief, we take the solution of L-TMMA [10]
as the starting point to develop a new algorithm that utmostly
reduces the exchanged messages and the hardware complexity
without any performance loss. Meanwhile, a well-optimized

1) (q − 1) 2-minimum ﬁnders are replaced by the (q − 1)

A. Proposed Architecture
Based on the modiﬁed L-TMMA shown in Alg. 3, a well-
optimized decoder architecture is proposed in this section.
Figure 2 shows the top-level architecture of the CNU. The
data ﬂow and data width are illustrated as well. A check node

operators, the proposed CNU is composed of six modules: 1)
N2D;2 ) 1-min ﬁnder ;3 ) L-min ﬁnder ;4 ) L-m2-col ﬁnder ;
5) L-transfer; and 6) E-I generator.T h e N2D is the same as
the previous works [5], [10]. The other ﬁve modules will be

1-min ﬁnder: In this module, the tree structure is adopted

parators or multiplexers is (d

IV . C
ONCLUSION
In this brief, we have presented a novel CNU architecture
for the TMM decoder of NB-LDPC codes. Many optimization
techniques, specially smart algorithmic transformation and
novel architectural schemes, have been introduced and applied
to this design. Synthesis results demonstrate the advantages of
these techniques, and show that the proposed design is more
efﬁcient than prior arts.
